# DSC step (step A)

Proves that the document signing certificate (DSC) was issued by a registered CSCA key, valid at the DSC's issuance, and is not revoked. There is one circuit per CSCA signing configuration and `TBSCertificate` size bucket, generated by `rust/eid-vectors` (`eid-vectors circuits`) under `noir/circuits/dsc/<group>/<variant>/tbs_<bucket>`:

| group | variants |
|---|---|
| `rsa_pkcs1v15` | `<bits>_<hash>`: RSA-2048/3072/4096/6144 with SHA-1/256/384/512 |
| `rsa_pss` | `<bits>_<hash>_s<salt>`: RSA-3072/4096, MGF1 with the same hash |
| `ecdsa` | `<curve>_<hash>`: P-256/384/521, brainpoolP256/384/512r1 |

The configurations are exactly the 31 (CSCA key, scheme) pairs used by verified signatures in the registry data (`DSC_CONFIGS` in `rust/eid-vectors/src/circuits.rs`), including four with SHA-1. Buckets are 700, 1000, 1200 and 1600 bytes. The prover picks the smallest bucket its `TBSCertificate` fits: hashing cost grows with the bucket, not the actual length.

## Interface

```noir
fn main(
    root: Field,                    // registry root (csca-registry commitment), returned for the kernels
    salt: Field,                    // blinds the output commitment
    w: eid_steps::dsc::Witness<T, K>,
    // RSA:   redc: [u128; N], signature: [u8; K]
    // ECDSA: r: [u8; S], s: [u8; S]
) -> return_data [Field; 2]         // [root, commitment]: the record noir-zk's kernel reads (registry_root public, c_A the link)
```

`Witness` holds:
- the DSC `TBSCertificate` zero-padded to `T`;
- the CSCA key material `K` (RSA modulus, or EC `x ‖ y`);
- the CSCA leaf header and its Merkle path;
- the revocation root;
- the non-revocation witness.

## Statement

With `f = eid_der::parse_tbs(w.tbs)`, the circuit asserts:

1. **The buffer is a TBSCertificate.** `w.tbs` is a DER `TBSCertificate` of length `f.len ≤ T`, followed only by zeros. The version `[0]`, serial, signature algorithm, issuer, validity (exactly two `Time`s) and subject are walked with every bound checked against the outer length.
2. **The leaf matches the circuit.** The CSCA leaf header matches the circuit's key type, curve and size, so a leaf can't be reinterpreted as another key type.
3. **The CSCA key was registered and valid at issuance.** The key is a leaf under `root`, and its validity period covers the DSC's `notBefore`: the ICAO chain model (`csca_registry::verify_key`).
4. **The DSC is not revoked.** Its serial has no revocation leaf under that CSCA key (`csca_registry::verify_not_revoked`).
5. **The CSCA signed this certificate.**
   - **RSA:** `w.csca_key` is the modulus and `w.header.exponent` the exponent.
   - **ECDSA:** `w.csca_key` splits into `x ‖ y`.

   The signature is over `hash(tbs[..f.len])`, checked with `eid_rsa` or `eid_ecdsa`.
6. **Outputs.** The circuit returns, through the databus, `root`, and `commitment = H(salt, country, hash_id, f.len, pack_be(tbs))`, where `hash_id` (1 = SHA-1 … 5 = SHA-512) is the TBS hash, committed but not output. `kernel_dsc` folds it and makes `root` public ([FOLDING.md](../FOLDING.md)). The SOD step recomputes the commitment from the same `TBSCertificate` to read the DSC key.

## Review notes

- **The signature value isn't bound to the certificate bytes.** It's a witness: any valid CSCA signature over `tbs[..len]` proves the CSCA signed those bytes. The certificate's own `signatureAlgorithm` field isn't compared with the circuit's scheme, for the same reason.
- **Serials must be positive and at most 20 bytes, plus an optional leading `0x00`.** Negative serials are rejected. RFC 5280 forbids them, but a few issuers produce them, and such DSCs would need a separate path.
- **Only X.509 v3 is accepted.** A DSC without the `[0]` version field (v1) is rejected.
- **An empty revocation tree is accepted** (csca-registry v0.3.1): with no revocations, the exclusion witness is slot 0 holding the zero leaf.
- **Costs per country and circuit** are in [docs/COSTS.md](../COSTS.md).

## Tests

- **`noir/lib/steps`:** 33 real certificates, standing in for DSCs, pass `dsc::check` against the fixture registry. The witnesses come from csca-registry's own `prove key` at `notBefore` and `prove not-revoked`. The negative cases cover:
  - a leaf of another key size or type;
  - non-zero padding;
  - another root;
  - a `notBefore` before the CSCA period;
  - an exclusion witness reused for another serial;
  - a non-SEQUENCE input;
  - commitment sensitivity to every input.
- **Circuits:** every one of the 31 configurations has a `Prover.toml` built from a real certificate. CI runs `nargo execute` on all of them, and they were proven and verified with `bb` for `docs/COSTS.md`.
