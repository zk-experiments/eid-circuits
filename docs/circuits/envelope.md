# Envelope step (step C)

Proves that the envelope published with the proof encrypts the document's real DG1 and DG11 to the given viewer keys:
- the eContent (LDS security object) hashes to step B's `messageDigest`;
- it lists the hashes of exactly this DG1 and DG11;
- the document hasn't expired at the proof date;
- its issuing state is the country of the CSCA leaf from step A;
- the ciphertext is DG1 and DG11 under a key wrapped to each viewer.

There is one circuit per (eContent hash, data group hash) pair and eContent size bucket, generated by `rust/eid-vectors` under `noir/circuits/envelope/<econtent hash>_<dg hash>/lds_<bucket>`:
- hashes: SHA-1, SHA-256, SHA-384 and SHA-512, all 16 pairs (issuers don't always use the same hash for both);
- buckets: 512, 1024 and 1536 bytes (16 data groups with SHA-512 hashes take about 1.2 kB).

## Interface

```noir
fn main(
    date: pub u64,                                  // proof date D, unix seconds
    viewers: pub [EmbeddedCurvePoint; 4],           // Grumpkin viewer keys; (0, 0) leaves a slot empty
    w: eid_steps::envelope::Witness<E>,
) -> pub eid_steps::envelope::Outputs
```

`Witness` holds:
- step B's commitment preimage (salt, country, `messageDigest` zero-padded to 64 bytes, its length);
- the eContent zero-padded to `E`;
- the offsets of the DG1 and DG11 entries in it, and `has_dg11`;
- DG1 (95-byte buffer) and DG11 (512-byte buffer, all zeros when absent);
- the ephemeral scalar `e` and data key `K`.

`Outputs` is:
- `sod_commitment`: must equal step B's output;
- the eContent and data group hash ids;
- the `Envelope`: `E = e·G`, four wrapped keys, and 24 ciphertext fields.

## Statement

With `p = eid_steps::envelope::check(date, w, hash_oid)`, the circuit asserts:

1. **The eContent is an LDS security object** (`eid_der::parse_lds`):
   - version 0, or version 1 with `ldsVersionInfo`;
   - its hash algorithm is the circuit's data group hash (NULL parameters optional);
   - at most 16 `DataGroupHash` entries, each a single-byte data group number 1–16 with a hash of the right length;
   - DG1 listed exactly once, at `dg1_offset`;
   - DG11 listed once (at `dg11_offset`) when `has_dg11`, and never otherwise;
   - zeros after its DER length.
2. **Step B signed it.** The eContent hash (circuit's eContent hash) equals `messageDigest`, and `digest_len` is that hash's size.
3. **The data groups are the listed ones.** DG1's hash, and DG11's when present, equal the hashes listed for them.
4. **DG1 is an MRZ** (`parse_dg1`):
   - it is `61 L 5F1F L' MRZ`, with an 88-character (TD3, passport), 72-character (TD2) or 90-character (TD1, ID card) MRZ, followed by zeros;
   - its issuing state, normalised with `csca_registry::country_from_mrz` (`D<<` → `DEU`), equals the committed country.
5. **Not expired.** The MRZ date of expiry, YYMMDD read as 20YY, ends on or after `date`: `date ≤ days(20YY-MM-DD)·86400 + 86399`.
6. **DG11 is one element (tag `6B`) followed by zeros**, or all zeros when absent.
7. **The envelope** is `eid_envelope::seal(viewers, e, K, plaintext(DG1, DG11))`: see [noir/lib/envelope](../../noir/lib/envelope/README.md). The plaintext holds both lengths and the whole zero-padded buffers, so viewers get exactly the bytes whose hashes were checked.

The outputs let the aggregation check `sod_commitment` against step B, and a verifier refuse SHA-1-derived proofs by the hash ids.

## Review notes

- **Dates.** The MRZ date is taken as UTC and 20YY; no document valid today expires in the 1900s. MRZ check digits aren't verified: the data group is signed, so they add nothing.
- **The eContent type isn't read from the SOD.** Parsing it as an LDS security object is enough, since that structure is what the issuer signed. The data group hashes are compared as raw bytes at offsets the walk has validated.
- **DG11 is a single TLV.** Its inner structure isn't checked. Viewers get the bytes the issuer signed and parse them themselves.
- **Data group numbers.** A duplicate DG1 or DG11 entry makes the document unprovable rather than ambiguous.
- **Viewer keys** are public inputs; the verifier (the chain) must only accept registered keys. The encryption's own review notes are in [noir/lib/envelope](../../noir/lib/envelope/README.md).
- **Sizes.** DG11 longer than 512 bytes, or an eContent longer than 1536 bytes, is unprovable.

## Tests

- **`noir/lib/steps`:** `envelope::check` is run on synthetic documents:
  - LDS v0 with DG11 (SHA-256), and v1 without DG11 (SHA-1 eContent, SHA-512 data groups);
  - `parse_dg1` on TD1, TD2 and TD3 specimens (check digits match ICAO 9303).

  The negative cases cover:
  - expiry (the last valid day passes, the next second fails);
  - another country and another hash algorithm;
  - a DG1 offset at another entry;
  - hiding a listed DG11, or claiming an unlisted one;
  - padding after the eContent, and DG11 bytes when absent;
  - a wrong `messageDigest` and a tampered DG1;
  - an MRZ of another length;
  - output binding.
- **`noir/lib/envelope`:** envelopes sealed by `rust/eid-envelope` (with and without DG11) must be reproduced field for field, and each is opened by its viewers.
- **Circuits:** each of the 16 hash pairs has a `Prover.toml` from a synthetic document. They alternate LDS v0/v1 and with/without DG11, and CI runs `nargo execute` on all of them.
